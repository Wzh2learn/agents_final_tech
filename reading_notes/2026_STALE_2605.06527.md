## §1 Paper Claim

The paper introduces STALE, a 400-scenario implicit-conflict benchmark with 1,200
queries, and CUPMem, a prototype for structured write-time state adjudication and
query-time constrained readout. The authors claim that retrieving updated evidence
does not guarantee that it governs downstream behavior.

## §2 Problem Definition

STALE defines an implicit conflict when a later observation invalidates an earlier
belief through direct or propagated consequences without explicit negation. The two
types are co-referential Type I and dependency-propagated Type II.

Fact card 1:

- Claim: implicit conflict requires belief incompatibility plus non-explicit invalidation.
- Full-text evidence: `fulltext/2026_STALE_2605.06527.md` L197-L216.
- Quote: `mn |=K ¬vo (a)` and no later utterance explicitly negates or corrects `vo(a)`.
- Boundary: benchmark explanations encode the intended dependency; they are not
  independent human gold for every commonsense consequence.

## §3 Method Structure

STALE evaluates State Resolution, Premise Resistance, and Implicit Policy Adaptation.
CUPMem writes structured state deltas, separates observed and inferred deltas,
adjudicates old-state archival/unknown-current status, performs propagation-aware
candidate search, and restricts query readout to authorized current basis.

Fact card 2:

- Claim: CUPMem is a typed temporal adjudication pipeline, not a retrieval-only module.
- Full-text evidence: `fulltext/2026_STALE_2605.06527.md` L1921-L2040.
- Quote: query verdicts are `SUPPORTED`, `OUTDATED`, or `UNRESOLVED`; readout consumes
  write-time archival/invalidation decisions.
- Boundary: it assumes a predefined state schema and does not solve schema-free state
  induction.

## §4 Training Objective

No end-to-end trainable objective is presented for CUPMem in the main method. The
implementation uses prompted extraction/adjudication stages and a local sentence
embedding model. The paper reports a prototype and evaluation, not learned superiority.

## §5 Experiments

The benchmark reports a gap between updated-evidence retrieval and downstream action.
CUPMem improves State Resolution and Premise Resistance relative to its reported
baselines but remains weaker on Implicit Policy Adaptation.

Fact card 3:

- Claim: the best reported overall accuracy is 55.2%; CUPMem reports 68.0% overall in
  the paper table while action adaptation remains much lower than state resolution.
- Full-text evidence: `fulltext/2026_STALE_2605.06527.md` L330-L350 and L459-L499.
- Boundary: these are author-reported results under their model/judge setup, not our
  reproduction. Our Qwen3.6 local probes are not directly comparable.

The official judge is an LLM judge with hidden `M_old/M_new/Hidden Logic`. Its rubric
can reward awareness that the old state is invalid without requiring a specific current
fact, so our work separately tests constant-answer and empty-answer degeneracy.

## §6 Limitations And Review Risks

- STALE is generated and expert-edited, not a natural longitudinal deployment trace.
- Type II propagation sometimes depends on plausible commonsense rather than logical
  necessity.
- CUPMem uses a fixed state schema and an embedding retriever.
- The paper explicitly accepts retrieval/reasoning/generation entanglement in its
  end-to-end metric; our contribution candidate must therefore separate these stages.
- The official LLM judge is not human gold, despite a reported human agreement audit.

## §7 Mechanism Abstraction

Reusable components:

- source-role-aware user-state extraction;
- explicit versus inferred update separation;
- stale/unknown-current archival;
- propagation-aware candidate search;
- constrained readout and action grounding.

Boundary conditions:

- a compact state card without write-time arbitration is only a partial adaptation;
- query-time correction over raw retrieval is not equivalent to CUPMem;
- retrieval recall and state propagation must be measured separately;
- state-resolution gains do not establish action gains.

Misuse risk: calling a two-stage `old_state/new_state/change_type` card a new memory
method without comparing generic fact extraction, matched compute, and action behavior.
