## Verified facts

### Venue / track
- Conference: SANER 2027, 34th IEEE International Conference on Software Analysis, Evolution and Reengineering; Tue 9 - Fri 12 March 2027; Richmond, Virginia, United States (Virginia Commonwealth University).
- Track: Reproducibility Studies and Negative Results (RENE) Track. RENE covers (1) reproducibility studies and (2) negative/null results, plus the negative or reproducible aspects of previously published work (journal-first spirit).
- Track co-chairs: Fabio Palomba (University of Salerno), Qiaolin Qin (Polytechnique Montreal).

### Page limits / format
- Format: IEEE Conference Proceedings Formatting Guidelines. Title 24pt; body 10pt; LaTeX `\documentclass[10pt,conference]{IEEEtran}` without the `compsoc`/`compsocconf` option.
- New negative results or new reproducibility studies: must NOT exceed 10 pages (including figures and appendices) PLUS up to 2 pages containing ONLY references.
- The alternative 5-page format applies only to appendices to conference submissions or to previously published author work; it does NOT apply to our new negative-results paper.
- Violations of limits or format are desk-rejected without review.

### Anonymity
- Full double-anonymous review. Author names and affiliations omitted. Self-citations in the third person. No identifying acknowledgments. Anonymize project/repo names and identifiable links. Detailed rules live on the Research track page, which the RENE page references.

### Submission type (our case)
- Choose submission type "RENE: NegativeResult" (the other type is "RENE: Replication"). Select the RENE Track in EasyChair.
- Data Availability statement after Conclusions is recommended (not mandatory). After acceptance, the author list cannot change and must equal the submitted list; titles cannot change unless approved by the program co-chairs.

### Evaluation criteria (RENE)
- Depth and breadth of empirical studies; clarity of writing; appropriateness of conclusions; useful actionable insights; availability of instruments and complementary artifacts; underlined methodological rigor. A negative result must come from a lack of effect, not from a lack of methodological rigor (for example misaligned expectations or insufficient power). Papers must point out the instruments/artifacts they build on and provide artifact links (the only exception is reproduction on non-releasable proprietary data).
