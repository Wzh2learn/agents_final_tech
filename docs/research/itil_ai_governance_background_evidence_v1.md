## 3. OUR INTERPRETATION

`INFERENCE`: Stage E can be framed as an evidence-producing part of a service value system: its frozen corpus and scripts define an improvement input, while explicit claims and stop conditions provide accountability for what may be decided from that input.

`INFERENCE`: the value chain analogy distinguishes intake, method definition, deterministic analysis, governance review, and future experiment handoff without asserting that the project implements ITIL process certification.

`INFERENCE`: continual-improvement language supports retaining negative and unavailable results as inputs to future design, not silently replacing failed predicates or thresholds.

`INFERENCE`: oversight, transparency, and accountability motivate source receipts, exact denominators, evidence labels, and a zero-endpoint execution receipt.

## 4. OUR MOTIVATION

`VALUE`: the project needs an organizational vocabulary for why evidence is passed between evaluation, supervision, and future experimentation with frozen gates.

`VALUE`: ITIL-inspired framing helps distinguish operational stewardship from scientific validity: a service can be governed well while a scientific claim remains unproved.

`VALUE`: the framework analogy is used to organize work, not to claim compliance, certification, normative conformity, or superiority over another governance standard.
